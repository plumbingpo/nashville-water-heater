Keeping Your Franklin Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Whatever type of water heater your Franklin home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
