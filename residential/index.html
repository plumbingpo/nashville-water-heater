No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

No homeowner likes a surprise on the invoice. That's why Nashville Water Heater prices every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the vent and gas connections. You'll know the problem, what the fix involves, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod slowly dissolves, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, call for an inspection right away. Catching it early usually keeps a fixable issue from becoming a replacement.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually tells us. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one forces the issue. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Nashville Homes

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Nashville Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Most homes do best with one flush a year, and houses on hard water may need a visit every six months. We adjust the schedule to the age of your heater, how hard your household works it, and the mineral content of your water, which determines how fast sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule helps. A clean tank heats water faster with less noise, a healthy anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping gallons you never use hot around the clock. Nashville Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means matching the first-hour rating to your morning rush, rather than simply copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the flow rate in GPM against the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water needs. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up after two teenagers start showering every morning, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical capacity, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Nashville

Most repair calls we take come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The hot water runs out fast, the tank rumbles and pops, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Not every water heater problem is equally urgent. A bad element only means lukewarm showers. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and a slow leak at the base can soak into flooring before anyone notices. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas utility first. We come prepared to swap elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, add a thermal expansion tank when pressure is the culprit, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
